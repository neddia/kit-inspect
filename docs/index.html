A camera that decides whether a kit may leave the station

A fixed camera checks every fastener kit before it moves on. It counts the bolts, nuts and screws, catches foreign objects, reads the printed kit tag, and tells a simulated Allen-Bradley PLC whether to release the tray. Anything short of a clean PASS for the current cycle holds the tray, and so does a silent camera.

Five good kits went to an operator, mostly a nut tucked against another part. One was failed wrongly: a screw lay almost entirely under a bolt (tray 4 below). Median 120 ms per tray on a CPU. Every tray's result · Every experiment

  • Detect. A YOLO26n model trained on 487 labelled photos of real trays finds the parts and known junk. An OpenCV check catches objects the model has never seen.
  • Read. OCR finds the printed kit tag. The wrong kit for the order is a hold even when every part is there.
  • Interlock. A cycle-ID handshake ties each verdict to the PLC's request. If the vision heartbeat stops, the PLC drops its release permit on its own.
